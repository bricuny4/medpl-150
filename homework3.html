Reflection

I actually chose two different issues to communicate through my photomontages. The first is about colonialism. The concept is really the quote “the world is your oyster” and I wanted to explore how that quote was actually taken in reality with European colonialism and the dispossession of Native Americans, Africans, Asians, and Middle Easterners. So you have a photo of a plate of oysters and then the images inside the oysters are pictures of both resistance and acts of colonization occurring. And then over it you have the map of the world, and then two suns at each side. The two suns were inspired by another quote “the sun never sets over the British Empire” so I really wanted to explore the extent of colonialism. The second photomontage is more about how a camera (and technology as a whole) can never substitute the beauty in reality. And this is not to look down on photography; in fact without photography I wouldn’t have been able to actually make the photomontage so I do think photography is capable (and more) of capturing beauty. But it should also not stand in the way of appreciating the scene right in front of our eyes. So you can see a camera with a smaller photo of the subject, mostly of her face. And then viewers can see the full bigger image, which is made to be more majestic. So for both works I used layer mask, specifically to isolate aspects of an image. Like for the first photomontage about colonialism, I used layer mask to isolate the picture of the plate of oysters with the hand. And I also used layering to put all of the scenes/images of colonialism into the individual oyster shells. For the second image, I used layer mask for isolating the camera from its original photo, and also isolating the smaller camera shot of the girl in the base photo. For blend modes, I primarily used screen in both. In the first work I used screen to make the two suns at the sides of the image blend into the map background. As mentioned before, I included the two suns as a nod to the quote saying “the sun never sets on the British Empire.” For the other photomontage with the girl and the camera, I used screen to blend a photo of stars I found to make the base photo look more majestic and interesting to the viewer to enforce my idea about appreciating the scene in front of you.
